How to find the countries you've visited from your photos

If you've ever tried to remember every country you've been to, your photo library already has most of the answer. Each photo can carry the place and date it was taken. This guide explains how that works, why some photos are missing, and how to turn all of it into a trustworthy list.

What your photos already know

When Location Services is on for the Camera, your iPhone saves the place where each photo was taken along with the date and time. That information is stored inside the photo itself, so it stays with the picture even if you move it to a new phone. In the Photos app you can see it by opening a photo and swiping up: you'll get a small map showing where it was taken.

Going through thousands of photos one by one would take forever, but because every photo carries a place and a date, a tool can read all of them at once.

Why some photos have no location

Don't be surprised if parts of your library don't show up. Common reasons:

  • Location was off for the Camera. You can check this in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Camera.
  • The photo came from somewhere else. Pictures saved from messaging apps and social media usually have their location removed, and screenshots don't record one at all.
  • It was taken on another camera. A standalone camera or drone only records a location if it has GPS.
  • It's from before smartphones. Old film scans and early digital photos have no place data at all.

Check the result before you trust it

A location tells you where your phone was, not whether you "visited" a country in the way you'd count it. Look out for:

  • Flights. A photo out of a plane window may be tagged over a country you never landed in.
  • Layovers. If you spent two hours in an airport, does that count? That's your call, but decide it before you start.
  • Border towns and cruises. A photo taken a few steps across a border, or from a ship near a coast, may land in an unexpected country.
  • Photos you didn't take. If someone AirDropped you pictures of their trip, they may carry their locations.

The fix is simple: scan first, then go through the list and remove anything you only passed through.

Fill in what the photos can't

Trips from before you had a smartphone, trips where you took no photos, and countries you lived in as a child won't be in your library. Add them by hand, with a date if you remember one. If you want the list to be useful later, it's worth adding a short note for each place so the memory has something to hang on.

Think about privacy

Photo locations are sensitive: together they show where you live, work and travel. Be careful about tools that upload your photos or their metadata to a server. Whichever tool you choose, check where the processing happens and whether an account is needed.

Doing it with Wanderpaint

Wanderpaint reads only the place and date of your photos, on your iPhone, and paints your map from them. Nothing is uploaded, there's no account, and your photos are never changed.

  1. Open Wanderpaint and tap Scan my photos when it offers (you can also do it any time from the gear button on the Map tab: Settings › Scan my photos). Allow access to your photo library. Full access finds the most; if you only share selected photos, Wanderpaint will tell you it can see only those.
  2. Wait while it works through the library. The screen counts photos read and then switches to finding the places.
  3. On the review screen you'll see We found a number of countries. Check that Home is right, because trips are the days you spent away from home. Then go down the list and uncheck any place you only passed through.
  4. Tap Paint and the number of countries. Your map fills in, and each country gets the date you first went.
  5. To fix or add anything by hand, tap a country on the Map tab and choose Been here, Lived here or Want to go. You can add a date and a note.
  6. Open the Stats tab for your totals: countries out of 197, UN members out of 193, the share of the world's land, progress for each continent and new countries by year.
  7. Scan again after your next trip with Settings › Scan my photos again. Places you removed stay removed.

If none of your photos have a location, Wanderpaint tells you so and suggests turning on Location for the Camera, or adding places by hand. Rebuilding every trip with its dates from your photos is part of Wanderpaint Pro; the country map and stats are free.
