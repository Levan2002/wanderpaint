How many countries are there in the world?

The short answer is 193 if you count United Nations member states, and 195 or 197 if you count a few more places. There's no single official list of countries, which is why the number changes depending on who you ask. Here's what each count includes and how to choose one for yourself.

Why there isn't one number

Nobody holds a global register of countries. The closest thing is membership of the United Nations, and the UN lists 193 member states. But some places that most people think of as countries aren't UN members, and a few are disputed. So the number depends on the rule you use.

The common counts

CountWhat it includes
193The member states of the United Nations.
195The 193 members plus the two non-member observer states the UN lists: the Holy See (Vatican City) and the State of Palestine.
197The 195 plus Taiwan and Kosovo, which govern themselves and are recognised by some countries but are not UN members.
MoreLonger lists add territories and dependencies, such as overseas territories and island groups.

You can see the UN's own lists on its member states and non-member states pages. Recognition of Taiwan and Kosovo is a political question that governments answer differently, so a count that includes them isn't a statement of opinion, just a choice of list.

What about territories?

Places such as Greenland, Puerto Rico or Hong Kong have their own governments or identities, but they belong to, or are closely tied to, another country. Most "how many countries have you visited" lists don't count them as separate countries, though many travellers like to keep track of them anyway. If you count them, keep them in a separate tally so your country total stays comparable with other people's.

Is Antarctica a country?

No. Antarctica is a continent without a government or permanent population, governed by an international treaty. It doesn't count toward any of the numbers above, though people who've been there often mark it on their map.

Choosing a list for your own count

  • Pick one list and stick to it. A total only means something over time if the rules don't change.
  • Decide what "visited" means. Did you have to leave the airport? Stay a night? Some travellers insist you must have left the airport. There's no official rule.
  • Remember it isn't the same as land area. Visiting ten small countries is a different thing from covering a large share of the world's land, and both are fun to track.
  • Don't worry about being "right". Nobody's checking. What matters is that your list is consistent and means something to you.

A quick example

Say you've been to 40 places. Counted against the 193 UN members, you might have 38. Counted against a 197 list, which adds Taiwan and Kosovo, you might have 39 or 40. Neither number is wrong. They answer slightly different questions, which is why it's worth writing down which list you're using the first time you count.

Doing it with Wanderpaint

Wanderpaint tracks your travels against a list of 197 countries: the 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. The map also includes territories, which are shown separately and don't count toward the 197.

  1. Fill in your map. Scan your photo library from Settings › Scan my photos, or tap countries on the Map tab and choose Been here, Lived here or Want to go.
  2. Open the Stats tab. The big number is the countries you've visited out of 197.
  3. Underneath, Wanderpaint also shows how many of the 193 UN members you've been to, and the share of the world's land your countries cover.
  4. Scroll down for progress on each continent and the new countries you added each year.
  5. To track a place that isn't on the 197 list, such as a territory, mark it in the same way. It appears on your map and in Places, but not in the 197 total.

Wanderpaint's borders come from Natural Earth, a public-domain dataset that shows borders as they are on the ground. Disputed places are listed as countries or territories the way most travellers count them, and you can always ignore any entry you don't want to count.
